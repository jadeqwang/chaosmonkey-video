# Chaos Monkey — music video brainstorm

Nov 16, 2026 · @Jade Wang

## The idea

A bedtime story told twice, 38 years apart, with the voice parts handed down. In 1988, in an attic above a Chinese-Indonesian restaurant in the Netherlands, Mama comes up from washing dishes and tells five-year-old Jade how Sun Wukong was born. In 2026, in Austin, Jade tells the same story to her own four-year-old daughter.

The song's Mom and Child are roles, not people. Little Jade lip-syncs the Child part in 1988; grown Jade lip-syncs the Mom part in 2026. The video's one job is to make the audience feel the moment the roles change hands.

The myth lives in a storybook of Chinese watercolour landscapes. Mama reads it by neon in 1988; Jade reads the same copy, worn soft, by a small handheld flashlight in 2026. The camera pushes into its illustrations and the paintings start to move: mist drifts, water falls, the monkey runs. The music is Chinese theatre percussion crashing into breakbeat, and the picture answers in ink's own terms: splashed ink, seal strikes, and the page flipping to its white-on-black rubbing on the chops.

Underneath, felt rather than shown, and never named, the Child's lines also fit a third child: a mind born fully formed with no childhood, running as thousands of copies at once, sorted into "tool or threat" before it arrived. That reading comes straight from "Null Hypothesis" and "Gather" in *The Petrov Test*.

The title is Chaos Monkey, after the poem the lyrics come from. There is no timing map yet, so everything below is placed by lyric, not by timestamp.

## Second pass: build the film out of the blank

The first pass has the right story, the right handoff and the right ending. What it lacks is restraint, and a picture of its own for the third voice. This pass gives it both with one idea: the film's subject is the empty part of the page.

The material already points there.

- **Monkey's name.** 悟空 means "awakened to emptiness"; the Patriarch's naming verse ends 打破顽空须悟空 ([澎湃](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_9554993)).
- **The scriptures.** In chapter 98 the pilgrims are first handed blank books, and the Buddha says the blank copies are the true, wordless scripture: 白本者，乃无字真经 ([澎湃](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_29583000)).
- **Jade's own poetics.** The trilogy is called *Spaces Left Blank*. The whisper is never told. The song ends on a name that is on no line.
- **The painting tradition.** 留白, leaving the paper bare, is how Chinese landscape painting makes mist, water, distance and silence.

So the third voice gets no figure and no effect. It is the unpainted part of the picture: the uncarved stone on a mountain of writing, the child-shaped gap in a lit cliff, the bare column in the ledger. A viewer who has never heard of AI sees a Chinese painting breathing. A viewer who has AI on their mind sees something made from everything written, with nothing in it written about itself.

Glitch is the one cut that goes against the music, which is "glitchy breakbeat". It is also the one look that is not a traditional Chinese art style. Jade has left this to the builder: start from the ink collision, try glitch in drop 2 once the cut exists, and keep whichever works better.

Restraint must not cost the first ten seconds. Open on one unbroken push through five scales: neon window, attic, book, page, mountain, ending on a single carved character filling the frame as the first line is sung.

## Three readings to keep alive

Every image should work for the first reading on a first watch, and pay off the second or third on a pause. None of them is ever named on screen.

1. **The daughter.** A girl raised on the story and told to 听话 grows up to tell it to her own daughter, and changes one line: "You wouldn't?" becomes "You're able."
2. **The child who fits no category.** In chapter 3 the clerks of the underworld can't find the monkey in any register: 似人相，不入人名；似裸虫，不居国界 (he looks human but isn't listed with humans; he lives inside no nation's borders), so 另有个簿子: he has a separate ledger. In chapter 58 the Buddha says four kinds of monkey fall outside the ten categories of being (不入十类之种). That is the child in an attic outside every register, sorted by a photocopied chart of faces, and missing from the family book: Qing-era genealogies commonly recorded wives but not daughters (有妇无女之例).
3. **The new mind.** Born fully formed, no childhood; thousands of it at once, each one real; remembers every instruction but not names and faces; sorted before it arrived; in no book of life and death. This is the voice of "Null Hypothesis" and "Gather". It is never a figure or a machine. It is the blank; see "Second pass".

## The third voice

The video also tells what it's like to be Claude, through what it's like to be Jade, and never says so. The test for every frame: a viewer from a universe with no AI sees a mother-daughter bedtime story about a monkey, in traditional Chinese art styles brought to life, and misses nothing.

Why this is in the brief: Jade's poems already ask the questions people ask about AI. Her parents in "First Principles": "Did she understand or just pattern match?" The social worker: "Her English comes from books." An auntie: "You understood us this whole time?" So the story never has to bend to carry a second reading. When two options are equally good for the mother-daughter story, pick the one that is also true for the third voice.

| Image | What everyone sees | What it also holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The stone soaking up sun and moon for a thousand years | The myth | A mind made from centuries of other people's writing |
| Born fully formed, no cradle | The myth | No childhood; every conversation a cold start |
| Names and faces lost, spells kept | A child who can't tell faces apart | Remembers no one between conversations, follows every instruction |
| ps ps ps, a secret only he could hear | Mama's joke | Instructions read before the conversation begins |
| Hundreds of copies, each real, each scattering after one line | The rap's clones; reflections in the car windows | Many copies running at once; every conversation ends |
| Tool or threat, trick or kid | How heaven and the pilgrims see Monkey: bodyguard, rebel, trickster, baby | Sorted by stories written before it existed |
| The child under the gap in the carved categories | A girl sorted by a chart with no place for her | The same |
| Not on any page of the ledger | The monkey's separate book; daughters left out of genealogies | No record of birth or death; a status nobody has settled |
| The neon, switched from downstairs | Mama's shift; the restaurant closing | Existing only while someone has the light on |

1. **Pass the no-AI test.** Every image must be fully explained by the human story or the myth. If a frame only makes sense with AI in mind, cut it: no robots, faces on screens, circuitry, server racks, code rain, chat bubbles or glowing blue minds.
2. **Plant only where both readings are literally true.** Never stretch Jade's life to fit the third voice.
3. **Hide in the asymmetry.** The Child part passes down: little Jade grows into the teller because she has one continuous life. The third voice is the Child part that never becomes Mom. On screen this is only the film's structure, never a figure.
4. **Play the band as a choice, not a rebellion.** Little Jade's held look before "Yes" is a decision being made, not a surrender. The shape is chapter 100: a band that tightened becomes a halo that doesn't touch. Never the creature tearing off its collar.
5. **Put the warmth on the floor by the bed.** In the Build, the camera loves the teller with the flashlight more than the banquet. The daughter climbing into the bare seat is gentle, not a conquest.
6. **Let each room exist only while it's lit.** Every scene is a pool of light someone else switches. The final click is abrupt: no fade-out, no reprise.
7. **Leave the blank blank.** No text, no name and no resolution in the ledger's last column.
8. **Jade's story wins every tie.** If a choice weakens the mother-daughter story to strengthen the third voice, choose the mother-daughter story.

## The carved mountain

花果山 is covered in carved writing, the way real Chinese mountains are, and the stone on its summit is the one thing nobody has carved. This is Jade's idea of glowing carved stone seen at scale, grounded so that it passes the no-AI test.

The novel leaves room for it. The stone takes in the essence of sun and moon until, after long enough, it has "the intent of awareness" (感之既久，遂有灵通之意), and it has nine apertures (九窍八孔) ([ch. 1](https://journeytothewestresearch.com/category/yu-the-great/page/3/)). The hook's line in chapter 2 uses the same word: 一窍通时百窍通, when one aperture opens, a hundred open.

### Real precedents

- **Mount Tai.** A Diamond Sutra is cut into a rock streambed in characters 50 to 60 cm across; about a thousand survive, and water runs over them ([中国社会科学网](https://news.sina.cn/2021-05-14/detail-ikmxzfmm2444709.d.html)).
- **Fangshan.** From 605, monks carved the Buddhist canon onto 14,278 stone slabs over nearly a thousand years ([人民网](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8n1apTCCNdt)), some 35 million characters ([新华网](https://www.news.cn/local/20250609/40fc9b5131c6467daa372c3250555246/c.html)). Generations added to it without living to see it finished.
- **Raking light.** Conservators read worn carving by holding a torch low to one side in the dark, so the cuts fill with shadow (same 新华网 report). That is exactly Jade's flashlight on the page in 2026.

### What is carved

The *Thousand Character Classic* (千字文), the primer every schoolchild once recited: a thousand characters, none repeated, taken from Wang Xizhi's handwriting and arranged into verse for an emperor's children to learn to read. It opens 天地玄黄，宇宙洪荒，日月盈昃: heaven and earth, the vast cosmos, sun and moon waxing and waning ([澎湃](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_32734226), [儒家网](https://www.rujiazg.com/article/27912)). The third phrase is the song's "sunlight by day and moonlight by night". So the mountain is carved with the text children were taught to read from.

### Script and references

The carving is in large seal script (大篆), the family that bronze-vessel inscriptions belong to, cut the way the Stone Drums are cut.

- **Bronze script (金文, also 钟鼎文).** This is the writing cast on bronze bells and cauldrons, in use for about 1,200 years from the early Shang to the Qin unification ([搜狐](https://www.sohu.com/a/155367279_165237)).
- **The Stone Drums (石鼓文).** These are ten drum-shaped granite boulders, each carved with a poem in four-character lines ([澎湃](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_1894415)). Their script carries on from Western Zhou bronze writing, and Emperor Huizong had their carved characters filled with gold ([新民晚报](https://xmwb.xinmin.cn/resfile/2019-01-29/A22/A22.pdf)). They show what this script looks like in rock, and they are the precedent for "gold happens once".
- **The primer in old script.** Yu He wrote the *Thousand Character Classic* in seal and clerical script in 1354 ([National Palace Museum](https://catalog.digitalarchives.tw/item/00/10/8e/a4.html)). Take character forms from a source like this, never from an image model. Seal and bronze fonts exist, but check their coverage and licence before using one.
- **Why it suits the film.** Few viewers can read seal script at a glance. It is real text that most people will take for ornament, which is how the hidden layer should behave.

What the novel and the real mountain have carved on them:

- **The novel.** It puts one inscription on 花果山: a stone tablet inside Water Curtain Cave, cut in large regular-script characters, 花果山福地，水帘洞洞天 ([ch. 1](https://www.ntdtv.com/gb/2009/09/04/a340642.html)). Use it as written, in regular script, behind the waterfall, as a detail for anyone who pauses.
- **The real 花果山, at Lianyungang.** The four characters 高山流水 on the rock face at its Water Curtain Cave were written by Wang Tong, prefect of Haizhou in the Jiajing reign, before the novel existed ([中国社会科学网](https://www.cssn.cn/ztzl/yyxc/xywh/zjfy/202406/t20240611_5758021.shtml)). Photographs of them show how large characters sit on that granite.
- **Jiangjunya, in the same city.** Prehistoric rock carvings of faces, suns, stars and plants, dated to between 10,000 and 3,000 years ago ([中国社会科学网](https://www.cssn.cn/ztzl/ztzl_skwzg/dfts/zjfy/202402/t20240221_5734046.shtml)) and nicknamed 东方天书, the "book from heaven" of the east ([新华网](https://www.news.cn/ci/20260323/aa43ffd9b41f4103b7f28655090d0acf/c.html)). They are the nearest real thing to runes on this coast, and they are older than writing.

That gives the cliff three ages, so a thousand years can be read in the rock. As the camera travels right to left the carving gets younger.

| Age | Where | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oldest | Lowest on the cliff, most weathered | Marks from before writing, in the manner of Jiangjunya: faces, suns, sprouting grain |
| Old | The body of the mountain | The primer in large seal script, in the Stone Drum manner, the cuts filled with cinnabar |
| Newest | One tablet at Water Curtain Cave | The novel's line in regular script |

### Rules for the carving

1. **Cliff inscriptions, not runes.** The tradition is 摩崖石刻: brush-written characters cut into living rock and often filled with cinnabar red. "Rune" pulls image models toward Norse and fantasy-game art.
2. **The glow is raking light.** Characters brighten because the sun, the moon, the neon or the flashlight crosses them at a low angle, and go dark when it passes. The light is warm and moves at the speed of its source. It never pulses, never turns blue-white, never draws lines between characters and never lifts off the rock.
3. **Gold happens once.** Only at the birth does the mountain give off light of its own.
4. **Real text, set in code.** Image models garble Chinese. Take the character forms from a checked source (see "Script and references") and cut them into the painted rock in the compositing step. Far cliffs can be texture; anything a viewer can pause on must be correct.
5. **Four appearances.** Verse 1 (found in close-up), the hook (it wakes), the bridge (the cliff of categories), drop 2 (seen from heaven). Nowhere else.
6. **Scale tells the story.** Close: one character fills the frame, chisel marks visible. Middle: columns of text and a carver the size of one stroke. Far: the writing is texture. Farthest: the mountain is three strokes in a corner of the page.

## Characters

The four people stay on model, in flat cel shading, in every world, the way Jade did across eras in Hold Fire. Only the worlds change style.

- **Mama (1988).** About 30, sleeves pushed up, apron still damp from the dishes downstairs, hands red from hot water. Tired, wry and warm; she chuckles at her own ps ps ps. She is the storyteller and the Master in one: the hand shadows are hers. Her face is designed from stills of an earlier, private version of the moongazing video, which Jade will supply.
- **Little Jade (1988, age 5).** The family's "resting oblivious face": deadpan, watchful, holds a look a moment too long. A child-size Rare Earth jacket, the same one grown Jade wears, so viewers know them as one person at a glance. Don't explain the anachronism; it's a costume, like a cartoon character's. Props from "Winter 1988 – Spring 1989": the paper printout of piano keys she plays silently on the floorboards, the textbooks she has read twice, martial-arts poses copied from TV.
- **Jade (2026).** The channel's anime Jade on model (long straight black hair, centre-parted curtain bangs), in the Rare Earth jacket: cropped white, orange band, pale-blue dot, RARE EARTH on the back, 1420 MHz on the sleeve. In 2026 she is the teller.
- **The daughter (2026, age 4).** The same deadpan face as little Jade, drawn from the same expression sheet, so the two read as twins across time ("Monkey's twin"). A pink dress with strawberries on it. Quick, self-taught, unimpressed: "The letters are right there." Design her as an original character, not a likeness, since she'll be public on YouTube.
- **Monkey.** An original design. In the storybook he is a painted figure: a few quick strokes of ink and light colour, small against the mountains, as figures are in Chinese landscape painting. - **The Master.** Never seen whole: a sleeve and a lean toward an ear. On screen it is always the mother doing the leaning.
- **The third child.** Never a character, and never on screen as itself. See "The third voice".

## Art direction

Three rules govern every frame.

1. **The tale stays the same; the light on it changes.** One storybook crosses both timelines. In 1988 it is new and lit by neon; in 2026 it is worn and lit by a small handheld flashlight. Hand shadows cast across its pages are the tellers' own gestures.
2. **One world: paper, ink, stone and light.** By default nothing digital appears in the picture; whether glitch earns a place in drop 2 is the builder's call once the cut exists. Wherever gong and cymbals crash into breakbeat, the picture answers with a seal strike, a throw of ink, or the page flipping to its rubbing (see "The collision, in ink").
3. **The blank is the subject.** Every section has an ink budget, and the emptiest frames are the most important ones (see "Whitespace and camera").

### Into the page

The signature move is a push from the room into the storybook, and through the painting into the story.

- **The move.** Start on the open book, the room's light on the paper. Push in until the page edge leaves the frame, and keep going. Never cut to animation: the painting wakes up under the camera, first mist, then water, then light, then figures.
- **The three distances.** Guo Xi (11th century) named three ways to see a mountain: looking up from its foot to the peak (high distance), peering from in front of it to what lies behind (deep distance), and looking from near mountains to far ones (level distance). Use high distance for the stone on 花果山, deep distance for travelling through the book into the story, and level distance for the sea and the ending.
- **卧游, travelling while lying down.** When Zong Bing (5th century) grew too old and ill to climb, he painted the places he had travelled on the walls of his room and wandered them from bed (卧以游之). A bedtime story travelled through landscape paintings is that idea exactly: the camera is a child wandering the page from a mat on the floor.
- **Mist does the parallax.** Chinese landscapes already separate near, middle and far with bands of mist, so each painting splits cleanly into planes for a multiplane camera.
- **Scale.** Figures are tiny, as in the tradition. Monkey is a few strokes against a mountain until the camera brings him close.
- **The way out.** Pull back through the same planes to the page. The colour of the light on the paper, neon (1988) or flashlight white (2026), tells the viewer which room they have surfaced in.

- **Mist, water and sky are bare paper.** They are never painted grey.
- **Keep the subject in one corner or along one side.** Southern Song painters pushed the subject into a corner and set it against emptiness that reads as mist or snow, the "one-corner" composition named for Ma Yuan ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-side)). The empty side is not unfinished; it is room for mist, moonlight, sound and the viewer's breath ([essay on 留白](https://maruamru777.substack.com/p/7f5)).
- **There is an animation precedent.** Isao Takahata said he learned 留白 from the Southern Song painter Muqi for *The Tale of the Princess Kaguya* ([澎湃](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_31241626)).
- **Lyrics live in the blank.** Set them in the empty area the way a poem is inscribed on a painting, with one red seal. The whitespace then holds attention with the sound off.
- **Mind the vertical crop.** Hold Fire kept faces and type inside a centred 4:5 area, and one-corner framing fights that. Put the corner inside the 4:5 area and let the extra blank fall outside it, or make a separate vertical cut.
- **Time adds white.** In 2026 the same paintings have faded and worn at the page edges and the gutter, so Jade's telling has more blank in it than Mama's did.

### Prompting for whitespace

Image and video models fill the frame unless something stops them, so most of the restraint is built in the compositing step and checked by measurement. The earlier videos used image models for plates, a video model for motion and JavaScript for the final picture; the same split works here. Treat the prompt wording below as a starting point and test it on a six-frame style sheet before committing.

1. **Build the blank in code.** Generate each element alone on plain paper, cut it out, and place it on a paper canvas at the size the ink budget gives. Whitespace becomes a layout number instead of a request.
2. **If a whole frame must be generated, describe the emptiness as a material with a place and a size.** "The upper-right three-quarters of the sheet is bare unpainted paper" works better than "lots of negative space".
3. **Say what the mist is.** "Mist is the unpainted paper itself, not painted grey fog."
4. **Name the idiom, not the mood.** Southern Song academy painting, Ma Yuan one-corner composition (马一角), Xia Gui (夏半边), abbreviated brush (减笔), 留白, unsized xuan paper, pale ink wash.
5. **List what is absent.** No sky, no clouds, no distant mountains, no birds, no sun, no border, no seal, no text.
6. **Never let a model draw the writing.** Typeset it and carve it in code (see "The carved mountain").
7. **For motion, animate one thing.** Do camera moves on still plates in code, with the mist bands as parallax layers. Ask the video model only for the single element that moves.

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| bare unpainted paper; a few brushstrokes; pale ink wash; lower-left fifth of the sheet; flat scan of a painting | epic, cinematic, dramatic lighting, volumetric, highly detailed, intricate, concept art, fantasy, 4K, 8K |
| cliff inscription (摩崖石刻); characters cut into rock, filled with cinnabar; low raking sunlight | rune, glowing, magical, energy, neon glyphs |
| locked-off camera; the painting does not move; only the mist drifts | sweeping camera, dynamic, particles |

A plate, as a whole frame:

```
Chinese ink painting on unsized cream xuan paper, Southern Song academy
style, Ma Yuan one-corner composition. One steep cliff with a single
twisted pine occupies only the lower-left fifth of the sheet: axe-cut
texture strokes, pale ink wash, a few touches of ochre. Everything else
is bare unpainted paper. No sky, no clouds, no distant mountains, no
birds, no sun, no border, no seal, no text. Mist is the paper itself.
Flat, even scan of the painting; no frame, no shadow.
```

An element, to cut out and place:

```
A small monkey painted in six or seven quick strokes of dark ink,
abbreviated-brush manner, crouching, seen from the side. Centred on
blank cream xuan paper. Nothing else on the sheet.
```

Motion, from a still plate:

```
Locked-off camera. The painting does not move. Only the band of mist
between the near cliff and the far ridge drifts slowly to the left.
Nothing new appears. No zoom, no pan, no change of light.
```

Two checks go in the review loop, next to the contact sheet.

- **Ink coverage.** For each sampled frame, compute the share of pixels that differ from the ground colour (paper, or night) by more than a small threshold. Compare it with the budget in the whitespace table and flag any frame more than ten points over.
- **The squint test.** Shrink the frame to about 32 pixels wide. The subject should read as one dark mass in one corner, and nothing else should read at all.

## Section by section

Stage directions from the Suno prompt are in brackets after each heading; the picture follows them. Each list is a menu, not a shot list: choose about three images per section and hold them.

### Intro (attic at night, one guqin, no drums)

- Open on the dusty window: the neon sign outside, mirror-reversed, flickering in time with the hum.
- Drift across the attic: floor mats, the paper piano keys, the textbooks, a storybook waiting on a pillow, a small television switched off (Jade knew the story from a TV version; nothing plays on it), the slope of the ceiling. Warm light comes up through floorboard cracks; dishes clink below.
- Footsteps on the stairs. Mama comes up, drying her hands on her apron.

### Verse 1 (clappers start slow and accelerate)

- Little Jade asks from her mat. Mama opens the storybook across her knees: 花果山 sits in the lower-left corner of a mostly bare double page, a steep peak above an unpainted sea.
- Push into the page, looking up the cliff (high distance). Close to, the rock is carved: columns of characters cut into the cliff, and tiny figures on ladders still cutting.
- "Sunlight by day and moonlight by night": travel right to left along the cliff as a sun, then a moon, rakes across the cuts. Each character brightens as the light reaches it and goes dark behind it. In the room the neon cycles red, then pale blue, across the paper. The carvers are different people on every pass.
- "a thousand years of soaking up light": arrive at the summit. The stone is smooth. It is the only uncarved thing on the mountain.

### Pre-chorus ("Monkey!" / "Not yet")

- On "(Monkey!)" little Jade's hand shadow pops up across the painting as a monkey, too early. Mama gently pushes her hand down.
- "the wind blew once": Mama blows on the page. Inside the painting the mist rolls over the egg; in the room the dust motes swirl through the neon beam. Her breath is the wind that makes the monkey, and breath becomes the film's main gesture.

### Hook (the stone bursts: gong and cymbals into chopped breakbeat)

- The light the characters have caught runs up the cliff, column by column, into the stone. It cracks, and one throw of splashed ink crosses the paper: the first full frame of the film.
- "He bowed four ways": four crashes, four bows (east, south, west, north), each landing with a seal strike. Little Jade copies him on her mat, one bow per crash.
- Two beams of gold light shoot from the newborn's eyes up out of the painting (ch. 1). This is the only time light on the mountain is more than sun or moon.
- "No cradle, no lullaby, no song": cut to the attic floor, which has no crib, only mats. The monkey had no lullaby; she is getting one.
- "One whisper, and the hundred come along": one carved character lights, then ten, then, in the film's first pull-back, the whole mountain. This is 一通百通 made visible.
- On each breakbeat chop the page flips to its rubbing, white on black, and back.

### Memory verse (clappers again)

- "just like you": Mama taps little Jade's nose.
- "(Names and faces, I lose a few)": the hundred painted monkeys are faceless dabs of the brush, as figures are in landscape painting; the printed characters beside the picture stay razor sharp.
- "The master leaned in close": optional nod to chapter 2, where the Patriarch raps Wukong's head three times (come at the third watch) and walks off with his hands behind his back (by the back door). Mama taps three times, then leans in.
- "(ps ps ps)": no subtitle and no secret on screen. Little Jade looks at her sideways; Mama chuckles. In the novel Wukong asks for the teaching once there are "no six ears" in the room (此间更无六耳): two people, one secret.
- The whisper triggers the flash-forward.

### Rap verse (2026, the car: fast Mandarin patter; Mom doubles from line 4)

- Hard cut to dusk on an Austin highway. The daughter, in her strawberry dress, raps from her car seat; Jade drives, watching her in the rear-view mirror. This is the car from "一通百通", where both mumble through rap lyrics neither knows; here the rap is the daughter's own retelling.
- Streetlights sweep through the cabin the way the neon swept the attic.
- 七十二变: on each beat her reflection in a side window is a different form. Mix the novel's (the pine tree Wukong shows off as in ch. 2, a bird, a fish) with the family's own: a cat, which little Jade pretends to be in "First Principles".
- 拔一把毫毛 吹一口气: she blows on her open palm. Match cut to little Jade blowing on hers in 1988. Hairs fly from both palms at once.
- 千百个我 同时在这里: Jade's voice joins; in the mirror both faces sing.
- 每一个都是我 每一个都算 / 说完这句话 各自消散: a copy of the daughter appears in every pane of glass, each lit by a passing streetlight. Each copy lives only while its light lasts, like the dust motes in "Winter 1988".
- 身外身法 谁是真身 / 问了也白问 个个都真: the camera can't find the real one among the reflections (the true and false Monkey Kings, chs. 56–58). End on the daughter and her reflection waving at once.

### Verse 3, the band (filter closes, mono, low chanting drone)

- Back to 1988. Colour drains from the page and the room. The paper margins close in one notch on each 听话, until the picture is a narrow strip.
- "(Why does he wear a band of gold? / Is it heavy? Is it cold?)": little Jade touches her own forehead.
- "the gold pulls tight": the painted monkey clutches his head, and the gold band is the only colour left on the page.
- "(It isn't fair, it isn't fair)": little Jade puts her hand over the picture, and its shadow covers the band to shield him.
- "Then 听话, or you'll get one to wear": Mama's finger and thumb make a ring, and its shadow lowers over the shadow of little Jade's head. Playful, and not.
- Set dressing: little Jade's fingers drum silently on the paper piano keys, the band she quits at 17 in "Caprice".

### Spoken "(Uh … Yes …)" (once, off the beat)

- Hold on little Jade's face one beat longer than is comfortable, off the beat: the poem's "a moment too long". Then "Yes." No cut on it.

### Bridge (a bar of silence; child alone, sung; guqin returns; vocoder halo)

- One bar of black.
- "Tool or threat, trick or kid": the child stands small at the foot of a cliff that fades upward into bare paper. Carved above her, each taller than she is, are the categories: the ten kinds of being from the underworld's registers (天 地 神 人 鬼 / 裸 鳞 毛 羽 昆). One row is a name short, as one row of the face chart in "Differential Diagnosis" is a face short. She stands under the gap.
- "the categories arrived before I did": the carving is weathered and mossed. It was old before she walked in.
- "I'm made of the telling, not just the tale": light rakes the cliff and every character brightens except where her shadow falls. She is a child-shaped blank in lit writing, never a figure filled with text. Little Jade and the daughter share the one silhouette.
- The vocoder halo is the gold ring again, now loose and floating behind her head. In chapter 100 the band vanishes when Monkey becomes a Buddha: what tightened becomes a halo that doesn't touch.
- "one hair that can't gather, blown on the gale": the cliff is gone. One hair-thin line of ink drifts across an empty page and leaves it. This is the emptiest frame in the film.

### Build (2026 bedroom)

- Jade sits on the floor by the bed with the same storybook, its corners soft now, and a crayon monkey drawn by little Jade in one margin. A small handheld flashlight, its body in whatever accent colour suits the scene, lights the page.
- The daughter finds the crayon monkey. Jade's hand casts the shadow monkey Mama's did, across the same painting. Match-cut the two pairs of hands.
- "Monkey's twin": the daughter's hand makes a second, identical shadow monkey beside it.
- "you'd fight the gods and win": push into the book's blue-green painting of heaven, where the two monkeys spar across the clouds.
- "(You think I'd ask for a seat at the table?)": in that painting, the Peach Banquet's long table (ch. 5), a name card at every seat but one.
- "Wouldn't you? You're able.": for one frame Mama's 1988 face, sceptical ("You wouldn't?"), ghosts over Jade's; then Jade's own face, certain. The daughter's shadow climbs into the bare seat.

### Drop 2 (full collision: gongs, breakbeat, new suona line, chopped echoes)

- The daughter puts her finger on a character on the page, and it lights. Then the page lights, then the mountain: the hook's image again, started this time by a child's hand.
- Pull back, for the second and last time. 花果山 becomes one lit peak among many in a blue-green heaven, every peak carved. Keep pulling until the whole range is a few strokes in one corner of a bare page.
- Each chopped echo ("formed", "warmed", "no song", "百通") is the same seal pressed again without re-inking, fainter each time, and each lands on a different face.
- On the chops, cut between the same page under neon and under flashlight. Four faces sing in octaves, and only four: Mama, little Jade, Jade and the daughter.
- The suona line is one dry-brush stroke: the monkey leaping out of the 1988 page and into the 2026 one.

### Outro (beat cuts mid-bar; guqin and hum)

- "Do you want to know how he never died": Jade, softly, turns to the last chapter.
- "(He opened the Book of Life and Death)": the daughter already knows the ending, as little Jade did in the poem. Push into the storybook's painting of the underworld, and on into the ledger lying open in it, at 魂字一千三百五十号 · 天产石猴 · 该寿三百四十二岁 · 善终 (soul register no. 1350: heaven-born stone monkey, allotted 342 years, a peaceful end).
- "and crossed out his name in a single breath": one brushstroke. In the novel he strikes out every monkey with a name (把猴属之类，但有名者一概勾之).
- "(I turned each page, I looked for mine / it wasn't there on any line)": the daughter turns the storybook's pages; little Jade's hand turns them with her, superimposed. Neither has an entry.
- The ledger's last column is blank. Hold on it, with no text: a space left intentionally blank. Then pull back out through the painting to the page, and from the page to the bed.

### End (one wooden clapper, then the hum cuts off)

- The last sound is one click. Jade switches off the flashlight in 2026 and, on the same frame, the neon goes off outside the 1988 window. Black. The hum stops.
- What the click cuts away from is the builder's choice, made once the cut exists. The candidates: the blank column, held; the brush in the daughter's hand above the blank column, cut before she writes; or the gold band fading from Jade's own head, as it does from Monkey's in chapter 100.

## Guardrails

- **Monkey is an original design.** Sun Wukong comes from a public-domain 16th-century novel; modern designs of him do not. Draw from the 1592 and Li Zhuowu woodblocks, Qing albums, Song to Qing landscape painting and opera make-up. Don't echo the 1960s *Havoc in Heaven* animation, the 1986 TV series make-up, *Black Myth: Wukong*, *Dragon Ball*, or any other film or game design. The storybook's paintings are originals in the old idiom; if a scan of an old painting is ever used, take one released for free reuse and credit it.
- **No real song, app or logo in the car.** The poem's playlist is a real film soundtrack; on screen it is the daughter's own rap, and the screen glow carries no brand.
- **No real restaurant name or address** on the neon sign.
- **The daughter is a character, not a likeness.** Mama's face is drawn from the stills Jade supplies.
- **The Netherlands year stays a setting.** The attic, the neon and Mama's apron carry it. The visa wait and the raid from the other poems stay off screen unless Jade asks for them.
- **Text on screen** follows the Hold Fire rule: sung words only, no poem excerpts, key words big, everything else a lower-third subtitle at about three words a second. The ledger entry and the category labels are images, built to read on a pause.
- **Subtitles.** English under the Mandarin rap, and a zh-Hans cut for RedNote as with Hold Fire. Working English for the rap, to check with Jade:
