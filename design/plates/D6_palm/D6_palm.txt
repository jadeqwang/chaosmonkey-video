A Chinese ink painting on cream xuan paper, wide format, mostly bare paper, after the old woodblock illustrations of Journey to the West in which the five pillars standing at the edge of the sky are the fingers of the Buddha's hand.
In the left third of the sheet exactly five colossal fingers rise straight up out of a sea of cloud, side by side like five tall round-topped stone pillars: only the upper two joints of each finger are seen, the rest of the hand is hidden below the cloud. The middle finger is the tallest, the ring and index fingers a little shorter, the little finger shorter still, and the thumb the shortest, standing a little apart at the right end of the row. Each has one or two fine knuckle creases around it and a plain fingernail at the top, so that at first glance they read as weathered rock pillars and at a second glance as fingers. Exactly five, no other fingers, no palm, no wrist, no arm.
A low sea of cloud, drawn in small scalloped fine-line curls with bare paper inside them, laps around the bases of the five fingers and runs along the whole bottom edge of the sheet to the right, a thin band, like a flat plain of cloud. Everything above the cloud to the right of the fingers is empty bare paper: two thirds of the sheet.
Painted with the brush: fine baimiao ink line of varying weight, a light grey ink wash modelling the fingers, darker at the left of each, with a faint warm ochre tint; the cloud curls in pale line only. The tops of all five fingers are well inside the frame with a generous bare margin above; nothing is cropped at any edge.
No figure, no monkey, no person, no face, no rays of light, no sun, no mountains, no trees, no text, no calligraphy, no seal, no border, no frame, no page gutter, no worm holes or stains. Flat even scan of the painting on plain unsized cream xuan paper.
The attached old woodblock print is the traditional composition to follow for the five finger pillars standing in cloud (its left page); repaint it in this ink-and-wash hand as one wide sheet.
