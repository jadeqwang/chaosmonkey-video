An animation keyframe pose sheet: the same monkey performer painted eight times, in two rows of four, every figure the same size and the same design, each separated by generous bare paper so each can be cut out.
The monkey (one fixed design): Sun Wukong as a Peking-opera martial performer in his traditional yellow stage costume, lean and wiry with long arms, painted in ink and light colour on xuan paper in a free, fast, splashed-ink hand. Line: quick calligraphic black ink line of changing weight, broken and dry in places. Colour: the whole costume is one wet transparent wash of gamboge yellow (the warm plant yellow of Chinese painting, matte, pooling darker at the edges, leaving flecks of bare paper, bleeding a little past the ink line), never metallic gold, never shiny satin. Costume: a close-fitting yellow jacket and yellow trousers, scattered all over with small dark ink comma-shaped swirl marks (the monkey-fur pattern of the opera costume) flicked in with the brush tip; a soft round yellow cap fitted close to the head with one row of small round pompoms across the brow (yellow ones and two or three cinnabar-red ones) and a single small tuft on top; a yellow silk scarf knotted at the throat, its two loose ends flying; a black sash tied at the waist over a short yellow flap skirt with a dark ink edge; black cloth boots with thin white soles. No plumes, no feathers, no crown, no gold circlet, no armour. Face: the opera monkey face in a few fine lines: one peach-shaped field of cinnabar red around the eyes, with round lobes over the eyes and a point running down the nose to the upper lip, set on a white face (bare paper) with a fine black outline; small gold eyes with a black pupil; a beaked down-turned mouth line; small round ears. He carries a long thin dark ink staff, a little taller than he is, with a short gold band at each end. The only colours: gamboge yellow on the costume, cinnabar red on the face and a few pompoms, a touch of gold on the eyes and the two staff bands, black ink; everything else is bare cream paper. It must read as a painting thrown in ink and wash, not a costume photograph, not a doll, not the 1961 Havoc in Heaven cartoon, not the 1986 television series, not anime, not cute, not 3D.

The eight poses, left to right, top to bottom: 1. liangxiang (the held opera pose): standing on one leg, the other knee raised high, one hand held flat above his eyes to shade them, peering into the distance, the staff held horizontally behind the small of his back, scarf ends flying; 2. bowing deeply towards the left, seen in profile, fists cupped together in front of his chest in salute, staff tucked under one arm; 3. bowing deeply straight towards the viewer, the same salute, the top of the cap showing; 4. bowing deeply towards the right, seen in profile, the same salute; 5. bowing away from the viewer, seen from behind, the same salute; 6. crouching low on his heels, scratching the top of his cap with one hand, staff lying on the ground beside him; 7. standing upright and proud facing the viewer, fists cupped in salute at chest height, staff tucked under one arm; 8. perched crouching on the top end of his staff, which stands planted upright and alone, one hand shading his eyes as he looks out.
Everything on one sheet of plain unsized cream xuan paper, evenly lit, flat even scan of the painting: no shadow, no vignette, no ground line, no background, no border, no seal, no text, no labels, no numbers, no signature.
