An animation keyframe pose sheet: the same monkey performer painted four times in one row, full body, head to boots, each figure filling most of the sheet's height. Four phases of a formal Peking-opera bow made to the four directions, the same bow seen from four sides. In every figure the bow is the same: feet together and legs straight, he folds forward from the hips about forty-five degrees with a straight back, head bowed with the body; both hands are held together in front of the chest in the formal salute (baoquan): the right hand is a closed fist, the open left hand is laid flat over the front of that fist, the left thumb tucked, both forearms level and the elbows out to the sides. The hands are NOT pushed forward and the palms do NOT face outward: the joined hands stay close to the chest. The staff is tucked under the left arm, its length running back along his side, parallel to the ground.
CHARACTER
The four views, left to right:
1. Bowing toward the viewer's right, seen exactly in profile facing right.
2. Bowing straight toward the viewer, seen from the front, the top of the cap with its tuft and the joined hands at the centre of the chest clearly visible, the face tilted down but readable.
3. Bowing toward the viewer's left, seen exactly in profile facing left (the mirror image of figure 1, but the right hand is still the fist and the left hand still covers it).
4. Bowing away from the viewer, seen from directly behind: his back, the key-fret pattern on the back of the jacket, the black sash knot, the back of the cap, the elbows showing at both sides where the hands are joined in front of him, the staff under his left arm.
