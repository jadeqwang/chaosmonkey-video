A study sheet of Chinese ink painting on one sheet of plain unsized cream xuan paper, in the abbreviated-brush (jianbi) manner of Liang Kai, the sheet mostly bare. Top row: the same monkey six times as a tiny landscape figure, each no taller than a thumbnail (about one twelfth of the sheet's height), each made of only six or seven brushstrokes: one wet blot of ink for body and head together, four quick flicks for the limbs, one dry flick for the tail, and a single dot of cinnabar red for the face; his small round cap is just the darkest point of the blot, and his two pheasant plumes are two hair-thin curved flicks rising from it. No outlines, no fur, no eyes, no features, no headband, no band of any kind. Six poses: looking out with one hand raised to shade his eyes; bowing; running on all fours; leaping with a thin staff (one straight stroke); sitting hunched on a rock (one pale grey stroke for the rock); a somersault. Middle band: a troop of about twenty monkeys as single faceless dabs of the brush, each one blot with flicked limbs and tail, no faces, no plumes, no red, scattered loosely as if across a hillside. Bottom right: one larger figure, the monkey in the pilgrim years, in a plain undyed grey robe drawn in a few pale lines, crouching and clutching his head with both hands in pain, one thin bright line of gold around his head: the gold band, the only colour on that figure. No fold, no crease, no album seam, no border, no seal, no text, no signature. Flat even scan, no shadow, no vignette.
