The monkey (one fixed original design): a lean, wiry stone monkey as a Peking-opera martial (wusheng) performer, painted in Liang Kai's splashed-ink manner: the body and close-fitting monkey suit are one or two broad wet washes of dark splashed ink with soft bleeding edges, the limbs a few quick abbreviated strokes, hands and feet in a few fine lines. Head: a small round black skullcap from which rise exactly two very long thin pheasant-tail plumes, each one single tapering stroke arcing backwards. Face drawn in a few fine lines: a pale bare-paper muzzle and cheeks, a beaked down-turned monkey mouth, small round ears, and one peach-shaped field of cinnabar red around the eyes, round lobes over the eyes and a point running down the nose to the upper lip; the eyes inside it are two small touches of gold with a black pupil dot; only a few minimal black lines for brows, nostrils and mouth. At the waist a short kilt of tiger skin suggested only by three or four dabs of ochre with dark stripes. He carries a long thin dark staff, a little taller than he is, with a short gold band at each end. The only colours: cinnabar on the face, gold on the eyes and staff bands, ochre on the kilt; everything else is ink on cream paper. Original design drawn in the old idiom: not the 1961 Havoc in Heaven animation, not the 1986 television make-up, no gold circlet, no headband, no armour, no yellow tunic, not Black Myth, not Dragon Ball, not anime, not cute.
