A Chinese painting of night, ink and light colour on cream xuan paper, in one hand: fine-line baimiao outlines for the faces and hands with light colour washes, dark ink washes for the room, the paper grain showing through everywhere. The attic above a restaurant, 1988: sloped ceiling, floor mats, bare floorboards with thin bright amber lines of kitchen light between them; one pool of red neon light from a small dusty window (no readable letters). Faces drawn in delicate fine ink lines with a light flesh wash, as in a Song or Ming figure painting, not anime, not a photograph, not a digital render. No text, no signature, no seal, no border.

Medium close-up, Mama in profile facing right, sitting on the mat, leaning down over the open picture book on her knees and blowing gently across the page, lips pursed, eyes half closed; dust motes swirl in the beam of red neon light that crosses the frame. The rest of the room sinks into dark ink.

Mama, 1988: a Chinese woman of about 30, youthful, smooth skin with no lines, no wrinkles, no grey hair; black hair in a soft late-1980s perm, loose curls cut level with her jaw, not longer; thin round wire-rim glasses with very fine metal frames; a round soft face with full cheeks and a soft rounded chin, gentle slightly downturned eyes with soft lids, a small mouth with a wry closed-lip smile; a plain cream cotton blouse with the sleeves pushed up above the elbows, a damp grey-beige canvas restaurant apron tied at the waist with darker wet patches, dark loose trousers, white socks; forearms and hands pinkish-red from hot dishwater. Tired, wry and warm.
