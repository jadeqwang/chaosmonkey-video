An animation keyframe pose sheet: the same monkey performer painted eight times, in two rows of four, every figure the same size and the same design, each separated by generous bare paper so each can be cut out.
The monkey (one fixed original design): a lean, wiry stone monkey as a Peking-opera martial (wusheng) performer, painted in Liang Kai's splashed-ink manner: the body and close-fitting monkey suit are one or two broad wet washes of dark splashed ink with soft bleeding edges, the limbs a few quick abbreviated strokes, hands and feet in a few fine lines. Head: a small round black skullcap from which rise exactly two very long thin pheasant-tail plumes, each one single tapering stroke arcing backwards. Face drawn in a few fine lines: a pale bare-paper muzzle and cheeks, a beaked down-turned monkey mouth, small round ears, and one peach-shaped field of cinnabar red around the eyes, round lobes over the eyes and a point running down the nose to the upper lip; the eyes inside it are two small touches of gold with a black pupil dot; only a few minimal black lines for brows, nostrils and mouth. At the waist a short kilt of tiger skin suggested only by three or four dabs of ochre with dark stripes. He carries a long thin dark staff, a little taller than he is, with a short gold band at each end. The only colours: cinnabar on the face, gold on the eyes and staff bands, ochre on the kilt; everything else is ink on cream paper. Original design drawn in the old idiom: not the 1961 Havoc in Heaven animation, not the 1986 television make-up, no gold circlet, no headband, no armour, no yellow tunic, not Black Myth, not Dragon Ball, not anime, not cute.

The eight poses, left to right, top to bottom: 1. liangxiang: standing on one leg, the other knee raised high, one hand held flat above his eyes to shade them, peering into the distance, the staff held horizontally behind the small of his back; 2. bowing towards the left, fists cupped together in front of his chest in salute, staff tucked under one arm; 3. bowing straight towards the viewer, the same salute; 4. bowing towards the right, the same salute; 5. bowing away from the viewer, seen from behind, the same salute; 6. crouching low on his heels, scratching the top of his head with one hand, staff lying on the ground beside him; 7. newborn: curled in a tight crouch, knees to chest, arms around his shins, head just lifting, the wet ink of him still bleeding into the paper, staff not yet in his hands; 8. perched crouching on the top end of his staff, which stands planted upright and alone, one hand shading his eyes as he looks out.
Everything on one sheet of plain unsized cream xuan paper, evenly lit, flat even scan of the painting: no shadow, no vignette, no ground line, no background, no border, no seal, no text, no labels, no numbers, no signature.

The attached images are his canon design (model sheet, and one finished painting of him): keep exactly its cap, the two plumes, the peach-shaped cinnabar patch, the splashed-ink body and the lean proportions in every pose.
