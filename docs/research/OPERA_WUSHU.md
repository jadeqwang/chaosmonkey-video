# Opera and wushu: research notes for the kinetic sections

Research notes for *Chaos Monkey*. They cover Peking-opera (京剧) monkey plays, face patterns, costume, percussion and stage grammar, plus wushu staff forms and neighbouring folk arts. The aim is to give the film one consistent grammar (paper, ink, stone, light) and a Monkey that is **original**: built from the tradition's rules, not copied from any single actor, film or game.

**Confidence tags**
- **[S]**: confirmed in this pass from the source linked next to it.
- **[K]**: standard knowledge in the field that I did not confirm against a source this time. Check it with an opera consultant before it goes on screen.
- **[?]**: sources disagree, or I am unsure.

Most web sources on technical vocabulary (锣鼓经, 翎子功, 跟头) are blogs and Q&A sites. The serious references are print: 《国剧场面图解》 (1936), 《京剧锣鼓入门》, 《中国戏曲曲艺词典》 and 《中国京剧百科全书》. Anything stamped on screen should be checked against one of them.

## 1. 猴戏: monkey plays

### History and schools
- **Origins.** Monkey plays come from Kunqu (昆曲) and Yiyang (弋腔) staging of *Journey to the West*. They belong to the 武生 role type: there is no dedicated "monkey" role type, but the plays grew into a category of their own, 悟空戏, popularly 猴戏. [S] [北京日报 via 非遗网](https://www.ihchina.cn/news_1_details/10263.html), [文汇报](https://www.whb.cn/zhuzhan/yishu/20160204/49217.html)
- **Peak, ban and revival.** The form peaked in the Republican era. After 1949 the plays were banned as "feudal and superstitious". In 1954, prompted by Zhou Enlai, 翁偶虹 rewrote the old 《安天会》 (in which the monkey is subdued) as 《闹天宫》 (in which he wins). Today only a handful of plays are regularly staged, out of dozens. [S] [非遗网](https://www.ihchina.cn/news_1_details/10263.html)
- **The two schools, in one line each.** 北派 "猴学人" (the monkey imitates a human); 南派 "人学猴" (the human imitates a monkey). The north is more 写意 and stresses that Wukong is both monkey and god. The south is more realistic, and its make-up artists even glued fur around the face. Northern plays tend to be single scenes (折子戏); southern ones are multi-night serials (连台本戏). [S] [非遗网](https://www.ihchina.cn/news_1_details/10263.html)
- **The schools are not that tidy.** 李万春 described his method as both at once: first turn yourself into the monkey, then have that monkey imitate people ("人学猴，猴学人"). [S] [搜狐 "民国猴戏的南北流派解读"](https://www.sohu.com/a/197954185_99901623)

**Performers** (source for all of these unless marked: [S] [搜狐](https://www.sohu.com/a/197954185_99901623))

| Performer | School | What matters for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 郝振基 (Kunqu) | early north | Studied real monkeys; aimed for 形似, a physical likeness. Wore the 草王盔 (straw-king helmet) in 《安天会》. Face: white outside, red centre, the "一口钟" pattern, designed to fit his long face. |
| 杨小楼 | north | Aimed for 神似 (likeness of spirit) over 形似. Made the body small by drawing in the shoulders and bending the knees (拢肩、曲腿). Did not rely on tumbling. Gai Jiaotian praised how lightly he landed in the sea scene of 《水帘洞》. |
| 李万春 | north (Yang line) | Stressed kingly bearing. Face: 倒栽桃, red and white sharply separated, few black lines. Wore a 金纱帽 (official's gauze hat) in 《安天会》, reasoning that Wukong already holds the post of 弼马温. |
| 李少春 | north, with southern borrowings | Face peach-shaped overall, with Yang-style eyes. Added the floor spin 地蹦 and the scissoring rise 乌龙绞柱, and even a pole-vault move. 1938, Tianjin: 《水帘洞》 played together with 《闹地府》; the same year, 《十八罗汉斗悟空》. |
| 郑法祥 | south ("赛活猴" / "小活猴") | Called the plays 大圣戏 and refused the word 猴戏, because Wukong must not be "played small" [S] [文汇报](https://dream-video.whb.cn/zhuzhan/jujiao/20160414/54393.html). His face came from a **rubbing of a stone-carved 斗战胜佛 at 大佛寺 in Guangzhou**, painted differently for four stages of Wukong's life. Movement: "三掌四拳", with borrowings from taiji. Invented the 登云步 and 踏云步 steps after watching apes in the zoo. |
| 盖叫天 / 张翼鹏 | south | 1923 onward: a 《西游记》 serial running from birth to imprisonment under the mountain. In 《水帘洞》, a double-whip (双鞭) set piece. |

**On "神 / 人 / 猴".** I found **no primary source** in which 郑法祥 uses the formula "神、人、猴". The idea that Wukong combines a god's powers, a human's feelings and an animal's body comes from literary criticism of the novel [S] (search lead: [aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/97411.html)). Present it as a principle, not as a quotation from Zheng. His own core reading of the character was "大胆不羁，嫉恶如仇" (bold, unbridled, hating evil). [S] [搜狐](https://www.sohu.com/a/197954185_99901623)

### Key plays
- **《安天会》 / 《闹天宫》.** Havoc in heaven: the peach banquet, fights with 天兵天将, the duel with 哪吒 (in Gai's version, against a pipa and the 乾坤圈). [S] above. The 1954 rewrite is the version that survives today.
- **《水帘洞》.** Usually includes 闹龙宫 (taking the staff from the Dragon King) and the sea fight. [S]
- **《闹地府》.** Wukong storms the underworld court and crosses out his own name, then every monkey name, in the 生死簿 (ledger of life and death); in the novel he says "一概勾之". Li Shaochun played it after 《水帘洞》 in 1938. [S] for the plot ([novel summary](https://www.sohu.com/a/313649563_100295177)) and the 1938 pairing ([搜狐](https://www.sohu.com/a/197954185_99901623)).
  - **Staging [?].** I could not find a description of how the ledger scene is staged. The normal opera convention [K] would be: the 判官 (judge, painted face) presents a ledger prop (a folded booklet or a scroll); Wukong snatches the 判官笔 (brush) and strikes names out with big calligraphic gestures; the strokes are probably punctuated by single gong hits. **Treat this as unconfirmed.** Watching a recording of 《闹地府》 (Shanghai or Tianjin troupes) would settle it.
- **《金钱豹》.** A demon-leopard play with a famous fork-throwing (飞叉) set piece; Wukong enters to subdue the leopard. The leopard's face is an 象形脸 (animal-shape face). [K]
- **《十八罗汉斗悟空》** (1938, Li Shaochun) [S]. Li Wanchun staged 《十八罗汉收大鹏》 the same year. [S]
- **《真假美猴王》.** Real and false monkey kings in mirror-image fights; Yan Qinggu revived it in 2016. [S] [非遗网](https://www.ihchina.cn/news_1_details/10263.html). This is the natural precedent for the clone and seventy-two-transformations imagery.

### Movement vocabulary of the monkey role
- **Body.** Arms and legs bent, shoulders hunched; this is what separates monkey movement from ordinary 武生 movement [S] [非遗网](https://www.ihchina.cn/news_1_details/10263.html). Yang's way of shrinking the frame: 拢肩、曲腿 [S].
- **Gestures that read as "monkey" [K]:**
  - 抓耳挠腮: scratching ears and cheeks.
  - Quick blinking and darting eyes (眨眼, 挤眼).
  - **手搭凉棚 / 搭凉棚**: the flat hand shading the brow while scanning far away. This is the most quoted monkey pose of all.
  - The "monkey paw": wrist dropped and fingers bunched, 刁手 / 勾手.
  - Crouching (蹲) with the weight on the balls of the feet; quick small steps; head cocking sideways.
- **Tumbling the monkey uses.** 地蹦 and 乌龙绞柱 (Li Shaochun) [S]; 旋子 (horizontal butterfly spin), 小翻 (back handspring), 虎跳 (cartwheel), 前扑 [K]. The term **窜毛** [?]: I could not confirm a precise definition this pass. It is usually described as a diving roll forward over or through something. Check before using it on screen.
- **出手 / 打出手** [K]. Throwing weapons at a performer, who kicks or bats them back. It is most associated with 武旦 roles, but battle scenes of 《闹天宫》-type plays use it as Wukong fends off the heavenly soldiers.
- **Staff play** [K]. 耍棍 / 棍花: spinning figure-eights in front of and behind the body, rolling the staff over the neck, tossing and catching it. See §6 for the wushu terms.
- **What makes a performance good.** The sources agree: avoid mere mimicry. Yang Xiaolou and Li Wanchun ranked kingly bearing (猴王气质) and 神似 above tumbling and realistic monkey business [S]. Zheng's "do not play him small" points the same way from the south [S].

## 2. 孙悟空 脸谱: the monkey face

### The named monkey patterns
All three names below exist, but **each is tied to a particular actor's face**, not to a fixed universal template. One source says the outlines were adjusted to each performer's face shape. [S] [搜狐](https://www.sohu.com/a/197954185_99901623); search lead: [wenwuchina 猴戏脸谱介绍](http://www.wenwuchina.com/a/203/257936.html)

| Name | Associated with | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **一口钟** ("a bell") | 郝振基 | White outside, red centre; the red area is bell-shaped, long, to suit a long face. One source says Yang Xiaolou also used it [?]. |
| **倒栽桃** ("peach planted upside down") | 李万春 | Red peach shape, round at the top and pointed at the bottom; red and white cleanly separated; black lines kept to a minimum. Some sources also file Zheng Faxiang's southern face under this name, but it looks very different. |
| 倒栽桃 + 檄(橄)榄形 | 杨小楼 | Combines the peach with an olive shape; replaced the soft eye shading with **dense eye rings plus brows** (浓眼圈加眉子). |
| **反葫芦** ("reversed gourd") | 李少春 | So says one source; another describes his face as peach-shaped overall with Yang-style eyes [?]. |

### Grammar common to all of them (what to draw)
Synthesised from the descriptions above, plus [K] for the common layout. Check against photographs.
1. **Central red field.** A heart, peach or bell shape covering the area from the brows down to the upper lip; the eyes sit inside its upper lobes. The name of each pattern is really the name of the shape of this field.
2. **Ground around it.** White (or flesh) on cheeks, chin and jaw, so the face reads like a monkey's pale muzzle and mask. Gold appears as eye rings or on the brow area for divine status. Gold and silver are the colours of gods and spirits [K]; that matches the novel's 火眼金睛 ("fiery eyes, golden pupils").
3. **Eyes.** Ringed, either with gold or (Yang's version) with heavy black rings and drawn brows.
4. **Brows.** Short, arched or flame-like, often in black over gold.
5. **Nose and mouth.** Black lines turn the nose into flared monkey nostrils and the mouth into a small, down-turned muzzle with a short vertical line under the nose.
6. **Forehead.** Sometimes concentric arcs or a small mark. The 金箍 (gold band) sits on the hairline in pilgrimage-era plays.

**Rule for an original face.** Pick a new silhouette for the red field (it should not be peach, bell or gourd), keep the white muzzle, gold eye rings and minimal black. Zheng's precedent licenses deriving a face from **a stone rubbing**. That fits the film perfectly: our Monkey's face can be lifted from an invented rubbing on the carved mountain.

### Pre-1949 documentation
- **《升平署脸谱》**, the Qing court album (the 升平署 was the court theatre office, founded 1827). National Library of China, via the World Digital Library (WDL 3023). **97 faces from nine kinds of plays**, on silk, by a court painter of the Tongzhi reign (1862–74) or later; 47 leaves, about 33 × 48 cm, so roughly two faces per leaf. [S] [Wikimedia Commons, WDL3023](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Office_of_Great_Peace_Album_of_Opera_Faces_WDL3023.pdf), [Asia Society on the 升平署](https://sites.asiasociety.org/education/VISIBLE_TRACES/rarebooks/rb22.html). I did **not** confirm whether this album includes a Sun Wukong face.
- **The Met's set of about 40 silk portraits** (c. 1900), one character per sheet: [S] [Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/peking-opera/).
- **Mei Lanfang's 缀玉轩 collection** [?]. It is often said to include face albums, but I found no source this pass. 缀玉轩 was the name of Mei's study. [S] [人民日报](https://paper.people.com.cn/rmzk/pc/content/202501/14/content_30052147.html)
- **Album layout** [K, partly S]. Court albums give each face its own panel or half-leaf. The face is painted frontal and cropped at the hairline and chin, sometimes with headdress. The character name, and often the play title, is written in small regular script (小楷) beside it. The ground is plain silk or paper with a mounted border. A grid with a label under each mask is faithful to the form; leave margins generous.

### General colour code [K]
- **Red 红**: loyalty, courage. 关羽.
- **Black 黑**: integrity, bluntness. 包拯, 张飞.
- **White 白**: guile. 曹操's **水白脸** (powdery "water white"). 油白 ("oil white") means arrogance, not treachery.
- **Yellow 黄**: ferocity. 典韦.
- **Blue 蓝**: fierce, unruly courage. 窦尔敦.
- **Green 绿**: outlaws and stubborn heroes.
- **Purple 紫**: steady, upright. 徐延昭.
- **Gold and silver 金/银**: gods, buddhas, spirits.
- **丑 (clown)**: a small white patch on the nose, the **豆腐块** ("tofu cube"). 蒋干.

Wikipedia adds only that white means one "does not show one's true face". [S] [zh.wikipedia 臉譜](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/臉譜). One source maps the five base colours to the five elements [S] [epochtimes summary, weak source](https://www.epochtimes.com/gb/3/12/9/n426374.htm).

### The bare face, and painting order
- **The unpainted face.** 生 and 旦 roles wear **俊扮** (also 洁面 [?]): a "handsome" make-up of white base, rouge, drawn eyes and brows, but **no pattern**. So a bare cell in a 脸谱 grid is not "nothing" to an insider: it reads as the human, unmasked role. For our film that is apt: the bare cell is the child, the uncast part. I found no source for "净面" as a term [?]. Do not use it.
- **Four classical techniques.** 揉 (rub the colour on with the fingers), 勾 (draw with a brush), 抹 (cover with white powder), 破 (break symmetry). [S] [zh.wikipedia](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/臉譜)
- **Typical order for a painted face [K, low confidence]:**
  1. Lay in the white areas: eye sockets, brow zones, muzzle.
  2. Fill the main colour (on our face, the red field).
  3. Outline in black with a fine brush: brows, eye rings, nose, mouth.
  4. Add gold and accent colours.
  5. Powder to set (扑粉 / 定妆).
  6. Re-strike the black.

  For animation, that gives: white → red field blooms → black line snaps on (on a gong hit) → gold eye rings last. Ask a 化妆 teacher before animating.

## 3. Costume and props on stage
All [S] from search summaries of Q&A and encyclopedia pages, including a 国家京剧院 (cnpoc.cn) page I could not open. Treat this section as medium confidence.

**Three looks** ([cnpoc](https://cnpoc.cn/cnpoc/gjkt/200902/ece77c901432482da3397980527be8fb.shtml), [360doc](http://www.360doc.com/content/22/1130/15/33911811_1058248414.shtml)):
- **King of 花果山.** Gold helmet with a pair of long pheasant plumes (**翎子**), yellow dragon robe (蟒), jade belt, thick-soled boots (**厚底靴**). The "monkey 蟒" version pairs the 草王盔 with plumes and **fox tails (狐尾)**; Liu Lingtong and Li Wanchun used real fox.
- **Havoc in heaven.** For agility the plumes come off. A close-fitting yellow **猴衣** (cut like 快衣快裤, embroidered with monkey hair and whorl patterns), a monkey cuirass, a yellow **钻天盔** helmet, thin-soled boots (**薄底靴**).
- **On the pilgrimage.** A yellow **猴罗帽** (soft cap) with the **金箍** band, yellow monkey jacket and trousers. The tiger-skin skirt (虎皮裙) comes from the novel ("腰系虎皮裙").

**Other details**
- **Headwear.** 郝振基 wore the 草王盔 in 《安天会》; 李万春 wore a 金纱帽 [S] [搜狐](https://www.sohu.com/a/197954185_99901623).
- **Plumes.** 翎子 are real pheasant tail feathers in pairs, with five to seven tiers of silk tassels at the base and a peacock-eye ornament [S summary].
- **The "凤翅紫金冠"** of film and animation is a modern blend: a 束发冠 plus plumes. Avoid it; it is the most recognisable borrowed silhouette [S summary, [zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/659652054)].
- **靠 with 靠旗** (armour with four back flags) [K]. When Wukong appears as 齐天大圣 in full armour, a yellow or gold 靠 is plausible but **varies by production**.
- **金箍棒 prop** [K]. Roughly chest to shoulder height on stage. It is shorter than the novel's description, so it can be spun. Usually lacquered red or black, with gold bands at both ends, sometimes in the middle too.
- **Public tradition vs one production.** Plumes, fox tails, yellow, the 猴衣 and the gold band are shared stage language. Specific helmet forms and patterns are per actor and per production. The TV-series and animated looks are not opera tradition.

**For our original design:** keep yellow, plumes and band as grammar. Do not use the 紫金冠 silhouette, the 1986 red-and-yellow make-up, or the 1961 film's peach-red face with green brows.

## 4. 锣鼓经: the percussion and its syllables

### The ensemble (武场)
The four core instruments, **四大件**:
- **板鼓 / 单皮** (small high drum) together with the **檀板 / 板** (clapper). The two are played by one person, the 鼓师 or 打鼓佬, **who conducts the whole band**.
- **大锣** (large gong).
- **铙钹** (cymbals).
- **小锣** (small gong).

[S] [zh.wikipedia 锣鼓经](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/锣鼓经), [搜狐](https://www.sohu.com/a/308373603_711530)

### Syllables: which character means which stroke
Mainly from zh.wikipedia [S], cross-checked with a common 360doc teaching note [S summary]. There are **regional and teacher variants**; the 1936 《国剧场面图解》 writes the gong as **藏** rather than 仓 [S] [澎湃](https://m.thepaper.cn/baijiahao_20533618).

| Syllable | Instrument and stroke |
| --- | --- |
| **仓** (cāng) | 大锣 struck. In tutti, 大锣 + 铙钹 + 小锣 together. **The big hit.** |
| **匡** | Gong and cymbals muted (哑音) |
| **空** | Damped (闷音) |
| **顷 / 倾** | 大锣 struck lightly |
| **才 / 七 / 切** | 铙钹, optionally with 小锣. Note: 七 is often used for the lighter cymbal stroke |
| **扑** | Cymbals muted (pressed together) |
| **台** | 小锣 struck |
| **令 / 另** | Light stroke **[?]**: wiki says light cymbal; the 360doc note says light 小锣. Avoid it as a "seal" |
| **大 / 达 / 答** | Single stroke on the 板鼓 |
| **八 / 巴** | Double stroke on the 板鼓 (two sticks together) |
| **嘟** | Drum roll (rapid alternation); written 嘟—— or 嘟/// |
| **龙冬 / 冬** | Soft and heavy drum strokes |
| **扎 / 衣 / 乙 / 一** | **板** (the clapper) or a rest-like light tick. 乙 / 衣 often mark an offbeat silence-with-clapper |

| **住头** | 大 台 \| 仓 才 \| 仓 — [S] 新浪 | Full stop |
| **搓锤** | 大大 大大 \| 衣大 衣台 \| 顷仓 乙才 … [S] 新浪 | Anxiety [S] 搜狐 |
| **脆头** | (软脆头 variant in the 新浪 blog) | Shame or shock |
| **九锤半** | — | Hurried, chaotic or furtive action |
| **马腿儿** | — | Fast three-beat cell; armed fights and swimming |
| **走马锣鼓** | — | Slower martial scenes |

Uses for 脆头, 九锤半, 马腿儿 and 走马锣鼓 are [S] from the 搜狐 page; their notation is not confirmed.

### The accelerating clapper before each verse
The closest true term is **撕边**: a drum roll that runs slow to fast and soft to loud, then lands on a gong. It is **played on the 板鼓, not the 檀板**. I know of no named pattern for the clapper alone accelerating [?].
- 导板头 is a lead-in to the 导板 aria type. It is not a general accelerando. Do not label the figure with it.
- If the visual should name the figure, use 撕边 and land the seal on the closing 仓.

### Notation layout (graphic material)
- **Modern teaching scores** run horizontally, with jianpu-style bar lines, as above [S].
- **Older handwritten 锣鼓经 and 工尺谱** run in vertical columns, right to left, with **板眼 marks** beside the characters [K]:
  - the 板 (downbeat) is marked with a dot, a short stroke 、 or ×;
  - 眼 (the weaker beats) are marked with ○ or 。.
  - The exact symbol set varies by school and period [?].
- 工尺谱 (gongche notation) is a "skeleton" notation [S] ([search summary](https://m.thepaper.cn/baijiahao_13381527)). It writes pitches as 合 四 一 上 尺 工 凡 六 五 乙 [K].

### Suona tunes (曲牌) for martial and ceremonial scenes [K]
- **《水龙吟》**: a ruler or commander ascends the hall, a grand entrance.
- **《将军令》**: mustering and dispatching generals.
- **《风入松》**: marching and battle movement.
- **《哪吒令》**: martial scenes.
- **《朝天子》**: court audience.
- **《泣颜回》** and **《粉蝶儿》**: martial entrances.

These belong to the 唢呐曲牌 repertoire; assignments vary by play. Confirm with a 京剧 musician before naming any of them on screen.

## 5. Stage grammar we can use as film grammar

### Poses and set pieces [K unless marked]
- **亮相.** A brief sculptural freeze at an entrance, exit or climax. The body arrives through a turn or a spin, the weapon or sleeve settles, then the head snaps the eyes front last, landing on the final 仓 of 四击头 [S for the cue].
  - Types include the entrance freeze, the exit freeze (often with a look back) and the group 亮相 at the end of a fight.
  - Rule worth stealing: **the eyes arrive last**.
- **起霸.** A general's solo armour-adjusting dance before battle.
- **走边.** Stealthy night travel hugging the edge of the stage. Examples: 《夜奔》, 《三岔口》.
- **趟马.** Mimed riding with a horsewhip (马鞭); the whip *is* the horse.
- **圆场.** Rapid gliding run in a circle with tiny steps; it reads as long-distance travel.
- **云手.** Hands circle past each other in front of the chest, like stirring clouds.
- **山膀.** The arm held out, rounded and firm, like a mountain ridge.
- **四功五法.** 唱念做打 (the four skills: sing, speak, act, fight) and 手眼身法步 (the five methods: hands, eyes, body, technique, steps) [S] [京报网](https://news.bjd.com.cn/2023/06/08/10457489.shtml).

### Costume techniques [K]
- **水袖功** (water sleeves). Commonly named moves include 抖袖, 甩袖, 翻袖, 拂袖, 绕袖, 投袖, 扬袖. Physics paper on 拂袖 and 绕袖: [S] [linstitute PDF](https://oss.linstitute.net/wechatimg/2019/03/qiuchengtong2018physicsgold.pdf). English Wikipedia lists the sleeves by function instead (hiding sleeve, angry sleeve and so on) [S] [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_sleeves).
- **翎子功** (plume work). Commonly cited moves:
  - 抖翎: the plumes quiver from a head tremor;
  - 涮翎: the plumes sweep in a circle;
  - 掏翎: the plumes are drawn down through the hands;
  - **叼翎 / 衔翎**: the plume tip held between the teeth; anger, resolve.

  I could not confirm these names in a source this pass [?]. They are standard in performance writing and Lü Bu / Zhou Yu plays.
- **髯口功** (beard work): 捋 (stroke), 抖 (shake), 甩 (fling), 吹 (blow) the beard.
- **甩发** (spinning a long hank of hair): fury or disaster.
- **靠旗**: the four flags on the back of the 靠 armour, which the actor flutters by shaking the torso.

### Combat and tumbling
- **把子功** (weapon routines) and **毯子功** (carpet skills: "跌扑翻摔", tumbling, diving, flipping and falls) [S] [京报网](https://news.bjd.com.cn/2023/06/08/10457489.shtml).
  - Named falls in that source: 吊毛, 抱背 (often written 抢背 [?]), 肘棒子, 锞子, **僵身**. The source notes 僵身 is often misread as "僵尸". It is a rigid backward fall, for example from a table in 《伐子都》 [S].
  - Other tumbling [K]: 前扑 (forward dive landing flat), 虎跳 (cartwheel), 小翻 (back handspring), 后空翻 (back flip), 旋子 (butterfly), **乌龙绞柱** (on the back, legs scissoring overhead, then springing up) [S that Li Shaochun used it], 地蹦.
  - **蛮子** [?]: some sources say a hands-free side flip. Unconfirmed.

### Face-changing (变脸)
Three basic methods, **抹脸, 吹脸, 扯脸**, plus **运气变脸** (changing colour by breath control).
- 抹脸: colour hidden in the palm is wiped on behind a sleeve.
- 吹脸: powder is blown into the face.
- 扯脸: layered masks are pulled off by threads; this is the most common method today.

Face-changing exists across many local genres, not only Sichuan. 净 and 丑 roles mainly use 抹脸 [S] [傅谨, 人民日报 2023](https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrbwap/html/2023-04/13/nw.D110000renmrb_20230413_2-20.htm). Peking opera has occasional 抹脸 moments, but the fast multi-mask 扯脸 is a **川剧 signature**. Do not present it as Peking opera.

### The stagehand and the bare stage
- **检场.** The stagehand who moves props in full view (there are no curtain breaks between scenes), feeds lines, helps with quick changes and spots dangerous stunts [S] [中国作家网](https://www.chinawriter.com.cn/n1/2019/0916/c419389-31354467.html). By convention the audience does not "see" him [K].
- **守旧.** The embroidered back curtain [K].
- **一桌二椅** (one table, two chairs). The same set becomes a throne desk, a court bench, a boat, a bridge or a mountain; what it is depends on the acting [S] [visitbeijing / 中国作家网 summary](https://www.visitbeijing.com.cn/article/47QkKdWBDl7). Standing on a chair or table = climbing a height; a table with chairs on top = a mountain or city wall [K].
- **虚拟性 / 写意** (virtuality, the freehand ideal). 齐如山: "国剧是歌舞并重的，它是'无声不歌，无动不舞'，而且可以说是舞更重于歌" ("national opera gives song and dance equal weight: no sound that is not song, no movement that is not dance; and dance may matter more"). [S] [搜狐, 齐如山《学京剧之入门方法》](https://www.sohu.com/a/374630829_488214). He worked the idea out theoretically in 《国剧艺术汇考》 [S] [珠海科技学院](https://music.zcst.edu.cn/2015/1010/c2076a91365/page.htm). His dramaturgy also included "不许写实" (no realism) [S summary].
- **Brecht.** He saw Mei Lanfang's demonstration in Moscow in spring 1935 and wrote "Verfremdungseffekte in der chinesischen Schauspielkunst" (1936; English as "Alienation Effects in Chinese Acting", tr. Eric Bentley, *TDR* 1961) [S] [Ronnie Bai, *Comparative Drama* 1998](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/compdr/vol32/iss3/4), [Saussy](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/journal/abstracts/saussy). Scholars doubt he borrowed specific techniques. Use him only as the outsider who noticed the actor "showing" the gesture.

### Who equated the bare stage with the painting's blank: 宗白华
In 《中国艺术表现里的虚和实》 [S] [爱思想](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/98182.html), Zong Baihua writes that the stage has no realistic scenery, only a few tables and chairs, and quotes an old performer: "戏曲的布景是在演员的身上" ("the scenery of opera is on the actor's body"). On painting:

> "我们见到一片空虚的背景上突出地集中地表现人物行动姿态，删略了背景的刻画，正象中国舞台上的表演一样。"
> ("Against an empty background we see the figures' action and posture concentrated and brought forward, the background left out, exactly like performance on the Chinese stage.")

He links both to 笪重光's 《画筌》: "虚实相生，无画处皆成妙境" ("solid and void give rise to each other; where nothing is painted, it all becomes a marvellous realm"). His image: 八大山人 paints one fish and "令人感到满幅是水" ("makes you feel the whole sheet is water").

**This is the citation for "blank xuan paper = bare stage".** Later scholarship builds on it via 一桌二椅 [S] [cssn](https://www.cssn.cn/skgz/bwyc/202504/t20250414_5869064.shtml).

## 6. Wushu: monkey staff, monkey fist, Ming manuals

### 猴拳 / 猴棍 (monkey fist, monkey staff)
Modern competition monkey forms derive largely from Emei and Sichuan lines (肖应鹏) [S] [Wikipedia, Monkey Kung Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkey_Kung_Fu).

**Body and hands [K]**
- Body shape summed up as **缩、小、紧、团** (contracted, small, tight, rounded): neck pulled in, shoulders raised, chest hollowed, back rounded, elbows bent, wrists hooked (**刁手 / 勾手**).
- Eyes darting.
- Tempo: long low stillness (crouch, scratch, look) broken by explosive bursts.

**Staff techniques [K]** (standard 棍术 names; 猴棍 uses them with monkey body and rhythm)

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 劈棍 | Overhead chop |
| 扫棍 | Low sweep, often in a crouch or spin |
| 戳棍 | Straight thrust with the end |
| 崩棍 | Snapping flick of the tip upward |
| 点棍 | Pecking strike down with the tip |
| 挑 / 撩 | Upward lift / scooping rise |
| 绞棍 | Small circles that wind the opponent's weapon |
| 云棍 | Flat circle over the head |
| **舞花棍** | Figure-eight wheels at the sides; **立舞花** (vertical) and **提撩花** (lifting-scooping flower) |
| 背棍 | Staff behind the back |
| 抛棍 / 接棍 | Toss and catch |
| 拨 / 架 | Parry / block overhead |

- 猴棍 adds rolling, crouching ambush poses (**蹲身藏棍**, staff hidden along the forearm), quick grip shifts to one end, and perching moves.
- **Climbing the staff and sitting on top** [?] appears in demonstrations and stage acrobatics (Li Shaochun's pole vault [S]). I found no standard taolu term for it.
- **搭凉棚** and "猴子望月"-type names [K] are common pose labels in monkey forms. Wording varies by school.

**Best in pure silhouette or a single brushstroke**
- 搭凉棚 (the hand and elbow angle reads instantly).
- 劈棍 freeze (one long diagonal).
- 云棍 (an ellipse of motion blur = one round 飞白 stroke).
- 舞花 (a figure-eight = a continuous brush loop).
- 蹲身藏棍 (a compact knot plus one horizontal line).
- 戳棍 (the shortest gesture, a dot plus a line).

### Ming manuals: pose names for labels
- **程宗猷《少林棍法阐宗》.** Part of 《耕余剩技》. Usually dated 1616; some sources say about 1610 [?]. About 55 illustrated postures with verse captions, which do not form one routine.
  - Confirmed names: **高四平势** (basic, flexible against spears) and **铁牛耕地势** ("iron ox ploughs the field": point high, strike low; read as "soft overcoming hard").
  - [S] [hk01 武備志](https://www.hk01.com/%E6%AD%A6%E5%82%99%E5%BF%97/680968/), [books.com.tw](https://www.books.com.tw/products/CN10084215). 茅元仪's 《武备志》 called it the fullest account of Shaolin staff [S].
  - Other names I recall (中四平势, 低四平势, 骑马势, 穿梭势, 滴水势, 边拦势) are **[K, unverified]**. **朝天势 not found** [?].
- **俞大猷《剑经》.** Despite its title, a **staff** manual, quoted by 戚继光 [K].
- **戚继光《纪效新书》.** 拳经三十二势 includes **金鸡独立**, 懒扎衣, 高四平, 中四平, 雀地龙, 当头炮 and 旗鼓势 [K]. "乌龙翻江" and similar poetic names belong to many later forms [?].

### Lines in space [K]
- **长穗剑** (sword with a long tassel): the tassel draws trailing curves, literally calligraphic.
- **九节鞭** (nine-section whip) and **绳镖** (rope dart): spinning circles, wraps and sudden straight releases. They are ready-made "drawn lines" for trails.
- **太极云手** (taiji cloud hands) is the slow cousin of opera 云手. Usable as the one-thing-moves gesture in quiet sections.

## 7. Other forms the film might quote

**皮影戏 (shadow puppets)**
- Figures (影人) are cut from scraped, translucent hide, usually cow; Tangshan and Luanzhou are traditionally called "驴皮影" (donkey-hide shadows) [K]. Dyed in transparent colours and pierced with fine 镂刻 openwork.
- Head and limbs are cut separately and joined with thread [S]. Typically about 11 pieces, worked with three rods: one at the neck, one at each hand [K].
- **Heads (头茬) are detachable and swap onto bodies.** One 辽南 troupe kept *hundreds* of heads and dozens of bodies to combine freely [S] [非遗网](https://www.ihchina.cn/news_1_details/11028.html).
- Styles [K]:
  - 华县 (Shaanxi; now 华州): fine, delicate cutting, sung to 碗碗腔;
  - 滦州 / 唐山: bolder, stylised profile faces with hollow openwork;
  - 成都 (Sichuan): larger figures.
- The screen is a 亮子 lit from behind [K].
- **For the film.** Swappable heads give seventy-two transformations; a shelf of identical bodies gives the hair-clones. Coloured hide lit through paper stays inside the paper-and-light world.

**木偶 (puppets) [K].** 提线 (string, e.g. Quanzhou), 杖头 (rod), 布袋 (glove, Zhangzhou). Glove puppets have a famous Monkey repertoire. Use only as a reference.

**剪纸 (paper-cut)**
- 蔚县: carved with a knife (not scissors) from xuan paper, then **dyed in dotted colours (点彩)**. Mainly 阴刻 (intaglio cut). The only paper-cut tradition with heavy colour. Opera faces are a staple subject. UNESCO-listed 2009 [S] [非遗网](https://www.ihchina.cn/project_details/13935.html).
- 陕北: single-colour red, bold folk shapes [K].

**年画 (woodblock New Year prints)**
- A key line block plus one block per colour, printed in turn and aligned (**套印 / 套色**).
- Places: 杨柳青 (printed, then hand-painted), 桃花坞, 杨家埠, 武强. 朱仙镇: purely block-printed in water colours, "不染不画" [S] [非遗网](https://www.ihchina.cn/project_details/18300).
- **饾版** [K]: many small blocks, one per colour patch, for painting-like gradations.
- **拱花** [K]: uninked blind embossing. Both are famous from the Ten Bamboo Studio (十竹斋) of 胡正言 (painting manual 1633 [S] [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/63436); letter-paper book 1644 [K]).
- **Mis-registration.** Registration is **套准** (also 对版). Mis-registration is **套印不准** / 套色不准, colloquially **跑版** [K]. I found no 年画-specific term [?].
- What it looks like: colour fields shifted off the black line by a few millimetres, with a white gap on one side and an overlap on the other. That is a genuine in-world analogue of RGB split.

**拓片 (rubbings)**
- 擦拓 (wiped with a felt pad) vs 捶拓 (tapped with an inked pad) [S] [非遗网](https://www.ihchina.cn/ky_zx_detail/21997.html).
- **乌金拓**: dense, glossy black. **蝉翼拓**: pale grey, thin as a cicada's wing. **朱拓**: red ink, often the first impressions of a new stone [K].
- The carved image appears **white on black**. This is the film's "page flips to its rubbing".

**印章 (seals) [K]**
- **朱文**: red characters, raised. **白文**: white characters on red, cut in.
- Edges are deliberately chipped (**残破**, also called 做印).
- Pressing again without re-inking gives fainter, broken impressions. Seal paste is 印泥 (cinnabar).

**Ink techniques [K]**
- 泼墨: splashed ink.
- 破墨: one wet tone breaking into another.
- 积墨: layer on dry layer.
- **飞白**: dry, streaked stroke showing the paper.
- 没骨: "boneless", colour without outline.
- 指画: finger painting, perfected by 高其佩 (Qing).

**走马灯 (trotting-horse lantern)**
- A candle's rising heat turns a paper vane wheel at the top. Paper-cut riders hung from the axle cast shadows that chase around the outer screen [S] [中国作家网](https://www.chinawriter.com.cn/n1/2025/0213/c442005-40418003.html).
- 周密's 《武林旧事》 (Southern Song) describes lanterns with horsemen "旋转如飞" (whirling as if flying) [S, second-hand].
- The proto-zoetrope for a looping running monkey: as a silhouette, a cut-out frieze of eight to twelve monkey poses circles on the vane, and the loop speed is set by heat.

**拉洋片 / 西洋景 (peep-show box) [K].** A painted box with lens windows; the showman pulls strings to change pictures while singing, with gong and drum hits on each change. A historical precedent for image changes cut to percussion.

**连环画 (picture-story books)**
- The 小人书 is typically **64开** (about 13 × 9 cm, landscape). One image per page with a caption block below, line drawing (白描) in black, and a colour cover [K].
- Sun Wukong sets were published by 上海人民美术出版社 and others from the 1950s to the 1980s, among them 严东-related editions [S] [JTTW Research archive](https://journeytothewestresearch.com/2025/02/28/archive-49-journey-to-the-west-%e8%a5%bf%e6%b8%b8%e8%ae%b0-a-60-volume-lianhuanhua-comic/).
- **I could not confirm the "河北美术出版社 1950s–80s set"** [?]. Check 孔夫子旧书网 before naming it.
- Learn the format only: landscape panel, caption below, page number, thin border.

**手影 (hand shadows) [K].** Hand shadows thrown on a wall by lamp. The mother's hands could make a monkey head on the attic wall. That would be the cheapest possible "first appearance" of Monkey.

**糖画 / 面人 (sugar painting / dough figures).** Skip. Both fall outside the paper/ink/stone/light world.

## 8. Ten sharpest ideas for this film

1. **Seals stamped on the syllables.**
   - *Picture:* each gong stroke lands as a cinnabar seal whose text is the stroke's syllable: 仓 on the gong, 才 on the cymbal, 台 on the small gong.
   - *Sound:* the drum's 八 / 大 lead-ins are not stamped. They show as a brush tip lifting above the paper.
   - *Authentic because:* those syllables are the drummers' own notation. Stamp only 仓 / 才 / 台 / 匡 / 顷 (unambiguous); never 令 / 另.
2. **四击头 → 亮相 as the only freeze in the film.**
   - *Picture:* every hook ends with the breakbeat chopped into the shape of 四击头 (答台 | 仓 — | 仓 才八 | 仓 才 | 仓). On the last 仓 the frame freezes into an ink painting, and Monkey's eyes arrive a beat after the body, as in a real 亮相.
   - *Sound:* the 四击头 rhythm inside the chop.
   - *Authentic because:* that is exactly what the pattern is for.
3. **撕边 as the verse accelerando.**
   - *Picture:* the accelerating clapper before each verse is drawn as a dry brush shivering faster across the page (飞白). It resolves as one splash of ink (泼墨) on the closing 仓.
   - *Sound:* the clapper accelerando and the gong it lands on.
   - *Authentic because:* 撕边 is the traditional slow-to-fast roll that lands on a gong. On screen, label it as such rather than inventing a term.
4. **Mis-registration as glitch.**
   - *Picture:* in drop 2, the storybook page is shown as a 年画 printed from blocks. On breakbeat chops the red and yellow blocks jump off the black key line (套印不准, 跑版) by a few millimetres and snap back. A glitch made of wood and paper.
   - *Sound:* the breakbeat chops.
   - *Authentic because:* multi-block 套印 printing is real; this resolves the open question about glitch inside the one-world rule.
5. **The ledger, struck out on 撕边一锣.**
   - *Picture:* the underworld ledger scene: each monkey name is struck through with one judge's-brush stroke.
   - *Sound:* each stroke on a single 撕边一锣 (嘟/// | 仓). The rolls tighten with each name, as in the 野猪林 example where three of them shrink in duration.
   - *Detail:* the last column is the bare one, the unnamed. The stroke hovers and does not fall.
6. **The face painted by the drum.**
   - *Picture:* an original monkey face, derived from a rubbing on the carved mountain (Zheng Faxiang's method), is painted in true order. White ground on the bass; the red field blooms on 才; the black line snaps on 仓; gold eye rings last on a cymbal choke (扑).
   - *Detail:* in the 脸谱 album grid, the bare cell is a 俊扮 face: the human, uncast role.
7. **The shadow-screen clones.**
   - *Picture:* in the "千百个我" rap line, a lit paper screen; behind it, hundreds of hide figures with *one* swappable head passed from body to body on each hi-hat.
   - *Detail:* the 检场 stagehand (all black) is visible and conventionally "invisible". Perhaps he is the third voice's only body.
8. **The lantern loop.**
   - *Picture:* the attic's 走马灯. Candle heat turns a paper wheel of twelve cut monkey poses (crouch, 搭凉棚, 劈棍, 云棍…). The shadow runs the walls as a loop whose speed rises with the BPM in the build.
   - *Detail:* one thing moves; it is all light and paper.
9. **The staff as a single brushstroke.**
   - *Picture:* in the hook, staff sets are shown only as the stroke each move leaves: 劈 = diagonal, 云棍 = ellipse, 舞花 = figure-eight, 戳 = dot.
   - *Sound:* each stroke on a snare chop. The figure is visible only on 亮相 frames.
   - *Authentic because:* this is the 写意 principle; Zong Baihua's empty background, applied to motion.
10. **The rubbing flip, with chalk-white plumes.**
    - *Picture:* on breakbeat chops the page flips to its rubbing (white on black). Monkey's 翎子 (pheasant plumes) are the one element that survives both states, as white flicks.
    - *Sound and gesture:* on a suona phrase he does 叼翎 (plume in teeth) as the only close-up. Resolve, not cuteness.
    - *Authentic because:* plume work is real 武生 vocabulary and very far from any cartoon silhouette.
