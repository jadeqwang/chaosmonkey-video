### What each shows about rock language

1. Hongren/Sound of Spring: dry, angular, thin-line structure; ink on blank paper; strata stacked as flat planes; few cun textures; waterfall left as unpainted paper. Hongren-type austerity (subject is a lonely valley; not verified as a named Huangshan site).
2. Dragon Pine: a single huge twisting pine in the lower foreground, craggy rock behind in dry ink with pale colour; vertical scroll builds depth by stacking rock masses with pale mist breaks between them.
3. Mei Qing, Misty Valley: sparse spindly outline of rock stacks and bare trees on satin, wet pale washes; mist is the unpainted ground; layers separated by gaps of empty silk.
4. Mei Qing fan: dense cluster of pointed peaks in blue-green and ochre on gold, pines on summits; shows peaks as a tight flame-like crowd.
5. Shitao, Thirty-six Peaks: whole scroll of dense pointed peaks in thin dry-brush line, tiny pavilions and trees, rock faces drawn by contour with sparse hatching; peaks pile upward with tiny gaps.
6. Shitao, Double Ninth: wet blurred dots (diandian) and washes in ink and pale colour; soft mist-rounded hill, houses small beneath.
7-14. Zheng Min leaves: precise, dry fine-line (Hongren-like) cliffs; steep splitting crags with slender pines on ledges; waterfalls as blank paper; a few leaves have a single tall pillar peak with pines on top and waterfall below (leaf c) and a lone spike behind a gorge (leaf d); mist is blank paper; leaf a is a diagonal cliff with a small figure/bridge.
16. Mei Qing, Nine Dragon Pool: soft grey-wet rounded rocks, white waterfall cascading in basins through mist, a pine with coloured foliage and a white-robed figure; swirling, lightly tinted washes.
17. Ding Yunpeng, Heavenly Citadel: tall hanging scroll; summit as a pointed crest above a layered gorge; deep red strokes for foliage (cinnabar allusion), mist banks between layers.

## C. Rock language for a 3D flight (prompt-ready)

- Hongren: dry, angular, hard-edged outline; rock faces as flat tilted planes; very sparse texture; large areas of empty paper left as mist and water.
- Zheng Min: thin precise contour lines, vertical splitting cliff faces, slender pines on tiny ledges, waterfalls left as unpainted paper.
- Mei Qing: twisting, flame-like peaks that lean and writhe, softly swirling cloud bands wrapping between them, pale blue-green and ochre tint over grey ink.
- Shitao: wet dots (diandian) and loose blurred washes, mist-softened rounded forms, pale colour accents, tiny pavilions for scale.
- Rock is built from a few overlapping planes, each bounded by a dry outline, with only a little axe-cut (fupi) and light hemp-fibre (pima) texture; no photographic shading.
- Depth comes from separation by blank mist, not by perspective: foreground dark and inky, middle ground pale grey, far peaks barely a wash, each tier cut off by clean white paper.
- Cloud sea is flat white unpainted ground; only the tips of peaks poke out, lower parts vanish.
- Pillars rise vertically in a crowded cluster, uneven heights, narrow gaps filled with cloud rather than shadow.
- Pines are few, dark, twisted, lean sideways from ledges and summit tops, with flat tiered needle pads; one large foreground pine frames the view (Dragon Pine, Shixin-style).
- Waterfalls are strips of reserved white paper with dark rock edges, pooling in pale basins.
- Colour: mostly monochrome ink; at most pale indigo, ochre and a touch of cinnabar-red foliage (Ding Yunpeng); tint is flat and thin.
- Texture: dry brush on peak faces, wet ink pooled at the base of each rock mass, a few tiny moss dots along ridges.
- Scale cues: a tiny pavilion, bridge or robed figure on a ledge so the peaks read as monumental.

## D. Buddha's palm: what is real

Sources are mostly Chinese travel/encyclopedia aggregators; Baidu Baike was reachable only through search summaries (the page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch), so treat these as moderately reliable, not authoritative.

- 佛掌峰 (Buddha's-Palm Peak, also recorded as 仙掌峰) is an established Huangshan name: one of the "36 small peaks", on the left behind Tiandu Peak. Named because, seen from the Yungu Temple descent road below Baie Ling (白鹅岭 to 云谷寺), five craggy rocks on the summit read as a spread giant palm. Baidu Baike gives its height as 1690 m. Also seen along the Yungu cable car line with Tiandu, Lotus Peak, 天狗望月 and 羊子过江. Source: Baidu Baike "佛掌峰" https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E4%BD%9B%E6%8E%8C%E5%B3%B0/6699558 (via search summary; I could not confirm the five pillars' relative heights, spacing or pine placement from any source).
- Closest alternative palm-like view inside the western canyon: 佛手石 (Buddha's Hand Stone) in the 西海大峡谷, a single rock like one upright hand/finger-citron, beside the 如意松 (Ruyi Pine). Source: https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_24841486 . This is one stone, not five.
- "五指峰" in the Xihai (West Sea) is mentioned only by one 2018 outdoor-forum post (peak with stone pillars like five fingers pointing up). Single unverified source: https://bbs.8264.com/thread-5508360-1-1.html . Do not treat as established. The well-documented 五指石 (Wuzhishi, Guangdong) is unrelated.
- Other named views checked: 仙人指路 (Immortal Pointing the Way) is a single robed-figure rock between Yungu Temple and the Pipeng junction, one raised hand (Ctrip/Qunar summaries); 十八罗汉朝南海 is listed under the North Sea (北海) area (Chinese Wikipedia 黄山, https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/黃山) but I found no description of its pillar count or spacing; 梦笔生花 (Dream Brush Blooming), North Sea, a single tall pillar with a pine on top as a brush tip (Lonely Planet POI https://www.lonelyplanet.com/china/anhui/huang-shan/attractions/flower-blooming-on-a-brush-tip/a/poi-sig/1454107/356183 ; general knowledge for the form); 始信峰 holds several famous pines (about four of the "ten famous pines", per travel notes); 西海大峡谷 is a U-shaped canyon about 3.6 km long between Paiyun Pavilion (排云亭) and Bu Xian Bridge, with 石柱峰 (about 1477 m, a vertical stone pillar) as core mass and clouds rising constantly (Ctrip/Qunar summaries).
- Cloud sea (云海): >250 cloudy or foggy days a year; named West, East, North, South and Sky Seas; peaks stand like islands in the cloud, appearing and vanishing; Paiyun Pavilion is named because clouds are pushed away before it (zh.wikipedia 黄山; Qunar 6937426; Ctrip 4005841).
- Highest: Lotus Peak 1864 m, Guangming Ding about 1860 m, Tiandu Peak 1810 m (zh.wikipedia).
- Verdict: "Buddha's palm" IS an established Huangshan name, 佛掌峰, but it is a crest of five rocks seen from one specific viewpoint, and I could not find reliable documentation of its exact pillar heights, spacing, or pines. There is no established "five-fingers cloud-sea canyon" view. Treat any five-pillar palm design as an artistic reading anchored on 佛掌峰 (five crags), with the cloud sea, pines and canyon language borrowed from 西海 and the paintings above.
- Journey to the West: Tathagata Buddha turns his palm into the five fingers, which become the Wuxing Shan 五行山 (Five Elements Mountain: metal, wood, water, fire, earth) pinning Sun Wukong for 500 years under a seal reading 唵嘛呢叭咪吽 (chapter 7). It is sometimes called 五指山 colloquially (Hainan's 五指山 is an unrelated real mountain). This is the literary reading of a hand-as-mountain, independent of Huangshan.
